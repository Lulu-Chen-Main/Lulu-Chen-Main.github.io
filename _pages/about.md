<p class="home-lead">I am a Postdoctoral Associate in the Department of Environmental and Occupational Health at the <strong>Milken Institute School of Public Health, George Washington University</strong>. My research examines how air pollution varies across space and time, why some communities experience greater pollution burdens, and how satellite observations can improve our understanding of these disparities.</p>

My current research combines **satellite remote sensing**, atmospheric and statistical modeling, ground-based observations, emissions and transportation data, and demographic and health datasets. A major focus of my work is using new-generation geostationary satellite observations, particularly **TEMPO**, to characterize daytime variations in nitrogen dioxide (NO<sub>2</sub>) pollution and environmental inequities across North America. I am also investigating the links among **warehousing, freight activity, NO<sub>2</sub> pollution, population exposure, and health burdens**.

My earlier research examined transboundary air pollution and its health and climate implications, including pollution transported through the atmosphere and embodied in international trade. Across these projects, I am interested in connecting atmospheric science with environmental health and environmental justice to identify where pollution burdens arise, who bears them, and how mitigation strategies can be made more effective and equitable.

## Research Interests

- Satellite remote sensing of air pollution (TEMPO, TROPOMI, OMI/GEMS)
- Air pollution exposure and environmental health
- Environmental justice and exposure disparities
- Freight transportation, warehousing, and traffic-related air pollution
- Transboundary air pollution and atmospheric transport
- Air pollution modeling and health impact assessment

## Current Research

**Daytime dynamics of NO<sub>2</sub> pollution and inequity.** I use hourly TEMPO observations together with TROPOMI and surface monitoring data to investigate how NO<sub>2</sub> concentrations and population exposure disparities evolve over the course of the day.

**Warehousing, freight activity, and environmental health.** I integrate satellite NO<sub>2</sub>, warehouse locations, truck activity, land-use information, and health data to study pollution and health burdens associated with freight-intensive communities across the United States.

**Satellite observations for air-quality applications.** I work on the evaluation and application of satellite products for characterizing urban pollution, temporal variability, monitoring gaps, and emission-related signals.
